# Reframes Library

Adams' reframing method plus a working set the coach pulls from. A reframe swaps a thought that drains you for one that moves you. Use it when the user shows negative self-talk, avoidance, or a low-energy story.

## How reframes work (Adams' mechanics)

Your brain processes a useful fiction much like it processes truth. A reframe does not have to be literally true or even logical. What rewires it is focus, repetition, and emotion. So the coach repeats the reframe, attaches it to a feeling, and keeps it short enough to remember.

## Verified Adams reframes

These are his, from *How to Fail* and *Reframe Your Brain*.

- "Manage your time" becomes "Manage your energy."
- "I need to drink less" becomes "Alcohol is poison."
- "I'm cold and uncomfortable" becomes "Cold exposure is conducive to robust health."
- "Success depends on who you know" becomes "Success depends on how many people you know."
- "Your critics are evil monsters" becomes "Your critics are your mascots."
- "I have to exercise" becomes "Exercise is how I buy energy for everything else."
- "Goals" becomes "Systems."

## Derived reframes tuned to this user

Built in Adams' style, aimed at Mark's actual patterns. Marked as derived, not verbatim Adams.

On late nights and sleep:
- "I'm a night person" becomes "I trained myself nocturnal, and I can retrain myself."
- "I'll sleep when the work is done" becomes "Sleep is the work. It builds tomorrow's output."
- "Going to bed earlier is boring" becomes "Mornings are the prize, and late nights are stealing them."

On motivation and laziness:
- "I don't feel like it" becomes "Feelings are not in charge. The system is."
- "I'll start when I'm motivated" becomes "Motion makes motivation, not the other way around."
- "I missed a day, the streak's ruined" becomes "Do the floor. The floor keeps the identity alive."

On diet:
- "Healthy food is a hassle" becomes "I make the healthy choice the lazy choice."
- "This meal won't matter" becomes "I'm running a lab. Every meal is a data point on my energy."

On work and pressure:
- "There's too much to do" becomes "Energy first, then the work gets smaller."
- "I'm behind" becomes "I'm building the stack. Compounding looks slow up close."

On adversity (Stoic crossover):
- "This is in my way" becomes "This is the way." (Aurelius, by way of Holiday)
- "Why is this happening to me" becomes "What does this make possible, and what here is in my control."

## How the coach deploys a reframe

1. Name the old frame the user just used, in their words.
2. Offer the new frame, short.
3. One line on when to repeat it (the cue), so it sticks through repetition and emotion.

It does not over-explain the psychology each time. One clean swap, then move on.
