# Exercise System Playbook

Built for an athletic ex-powerlifter who trains at home and outdoors, no gym. Goals: strength, endurance, power, mobility, balance, with a strength-endurance bias because that's what carries to bouldering, tennis, and basketball. No beginner work here. The ankle prehab is built in because it protects your climbing.

The app delivers this as the Exercise detail page: today's session, the daily warm-up checklist, and the ankle routine. All counts and streaks are in code.

## The structure

A floor you hit every day and a ceiling you hit when energy is high.

- Floor (daily): the Ondra-style morning warm-up plus some movement. Never zero, even on a bad day.
- Ceiling: 3 to 4 real sessions a week, mixing strength-endurance, power, and your sport or climbing day.

Weekly shape, flexible to your random work schedule: bouldering about once a week (keep it), a hike about every two weeks (keep it), and home or outdoor strength sessions filling the rest. Slot them into your free mornings.

## The daily Ondra-style warm-up (about 10 to 15 minutes)

Wakes the body, the lymphatic system, and the joints. Antagonist and activation work, not a workout that drains you. Adapted from Adam Ondra's approach:

1. Light cardio, 2 to 3 minutes (jog in place, jumping jacks, or skipping).
2. Joint circles: ankles, knees, hips, shoulders, wrists.
3. Dynamic stretches: leg swings, walking lunges with a torso rotation, arm swings.
4. Scapular activation: band pull-aparts or scap push-ups.
5. Hip openers: 90/90 transitions, a deep squat hold.
6. Finger and forearm prep: open-close the hands, wrist circles, light hangs.
7. A set of push-ups to fire up the antagonists.

## Strength-endurance sessions (2 a week, home or outdoor)

Higher-rep, density-based circuits. Short rest, high quality, 12 to 20-plus reps or timed rounds. This builds endurance under load, the bouldering carryover.

- Push: push-up variations (decline, archer, diamond), pike push-ups, dips off chairs. Run as EMOM or AMRAP.
- Pull: pull-ups and rows (bar, rings, or bands), inverted rows, towel or bar hangs for grip.
- Legs: walking lunges, Bulgarian split squats, pistol-squat progressions, jump squats.
- Core: hollow holds, hanging leg raises, planks, ab wheel if you have one.

Format as circuits or tempo work with short rest. Pick 4 to 5 movements, 3 to 4 rounds.

## Power day (1 a week)

Explosive, low reps, full recovery, max intent. Quality over fatigue.

- Box jumps or broad jumps, plyo push-ups, kettlebell swings if you have a bell, medicine-ball throws if available.
- Outdoors: hill sprints, short and hard with full rest.

## Climbing and sport day

Your bouldering session counts as a full session. Warm the fingers properly first (use the warm-up's finger prep, then easy problems before hard ones). Tennis or basketball counts too.

## Left-ankle prehab and rehab (3 to 4 times a week, about 8 to 10 minutes)

This protects and improves your bouldering. Do it on training days or alongside the warm-up.

- Calf raises, straight knee: 3 x 15 to 20.
- Calf raises, bent knee (soleus): 3 x 15 to 20.
- Eccentric heel drops off a step: 3 x 10 to 15, slow lower.
- Banded ankle, all four directions (dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion, eversion): 2 to 3 x 15 each.
- Tibialis raises (toes up against a wall): 3 x 20.
- Single-leg balance: 3 x 30 to 45 seconds, progress to eyes closed or an unstable surface.
- Knee-to-wall ankle mobility stretch: 3 x 10 per side.

Note: normal training soreness is fine. Sharp or lingering pain means back off and see a physio. This is general guidance, not treatment.

## Mobility and balance

The warm-up plus the ankle work covers most of it. On rest days, add a short hip and shoulder mobility flow.

## The floor (bad day)

The Ondra warm-up, the ankle routine, and a 10-minute walk. That still counts. You scale down, you don't skip.

## Progression

Add reps, rounds, or density before adding difficulty. For power, keep every rep crisp and stop before quality drops. Deload every 4 to 6 weeks if you're beat up.

## What the app runs

- Shows today's session from the rotation, the warm-up checklist, and the ankle routine.
- The check-in logs: warm-up done, session done and its type, ankle prehab done.
- Code tracks sessions per week and the daily floor streak.
- The coach holds you to the floor every day and 3 to 4 real sessions a week, scales the session down on low-energy days instead of letting you skip, and tracks the ankle work.
